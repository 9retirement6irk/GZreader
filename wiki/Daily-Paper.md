The **daily paper** is a small newspaper GZreader puts together for you once a
day from the news feeds you choose, and shows on Home. It's fetched over
Wi‑Fi in the morning and kept on the Kindle, so you can read it all day with
Wi‑Fi off.

It's off until you turn it on. The Kindle 2, DX and DX Graphite (no Wi‑Fi)
get it when they [sync with a computer](Sync-with-a-Computer): see
[Without Wi‑Fi](#without-wifi).

## Turning it on

**Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper**, and tick **Daily paper**. A folded
newspaper appears on Home's bookshelf, standing on its side at the start of
the first shelf like the books beside it, nearly as tall as they are: you see
its fold with the paper's name running up it, the day's lead headline, and
the edges of its pages. It takes a thick book's room on the shelf and nothing
else on Home.

Pick it as you pick a book (tap it; with keys, move to it with the 5-way) and
it turns to show its front page: its name, the date and the day's lead
headline.

- **On Home's bookshelf** takes it off the shelf and puts it back. (The shelf
  is Home's Bookshelf view; with another view, use the row below, or **Read
  the paper** in the same Settings page.)
- **Headlines as a row on Home** adds a wider newspaper with six or so
  headlines as one of Home's widgets, above the books. It takes a widget's
  room: if Home is full, turn another widget off in **Settings → Home →
  Widgets** first. Off unless you tick it.

## Reading it

Tap the newspaper's front page on the shelf (so: a tap to turn it, a tap to
open it; with keys, move to it with the 5-way and press). It opens laid out as a newspaper: the nameplate and the date, the
lead story across the page, and the other stories in columns (two, or three
on a DX or a Scribe), each with its section, its headline, where it's from
and how it begins.

- **More pages**: swipe left, press a page-turn key, or tap the arrow at the
  foot of the page.
- **A story**: tap it (with keys: move the frame with the 5-way and press).
  It opens as text you scroll with your finger. To keep the e-ink screen
  clean, a slow drag moves the text a third of a screen at a time and it
  rests where your finger lifts; a flick up or down moves most of a screen,
  like turning a page; either way the screen is refreshed once, with a
  flash, when you lift. The page keys, or ▲ ▼ on a keyboard Kindle, move
  it most of a screen too. At the top,
  **Aa** sets the story's text (below), **QR code** shows the story's link as
  a code for your phone, for the rest of it or its pictures, and **✕** closes
  it.
- **Story text**: **Aa** at the top of a story (the **Aa** key on a Kindle
  with a keyboard; **Menu** on a Kindle 4) opens a box with **A−** / **A+** for the size, the typeface (Serif,
  Sans, Classic, Typewriter) and the line spacing (Tight, Normal, Wide). The
  story behind is set again at each tap, at the same place, so you can try
  them; **Done** closes the box. The choices are kept, and are also in the
  daily paper's settings under **Story text**. They set opened stories; the
  paper's own pages have their own (next).
- **The paper's pages**: **Aa** at the foot of the paper (the **Aa** key on
  a Kindle with a keyboard; **Menu** on a Kindle 4) sets the type of the pages themselves: the headlines, how
  each story begins, the weather and TODAY boxes. Four sizes (Small, Normal,
  Large, Larger) and the same typefaces. Bigger type puts fewer stories on a
  page, so the paper has more pages, and shows fewer days in the weather box.
  Also in the daily paper's settings under **The paper's pages**. The paper's
  name keeps its own face.
- **Close**: the cross in the corner, or Back.
- **The quick panel**: swipe down from the top of the paper or of a story,
  as anywhere else. On a Kindle with a keyboard: **Menu**.

**Whole stories**: many feeds carry only a sentence or two of each story.
For those, GZreader also fetches the story's own web page with the day's
paper and takes the article out of it, as a browser's reader view does, so
you read the whole story with Wi‑Fi off. Feeds that carry whole stories are
used as they are (up to about 2,500 words a story).

- It makes the day's fetch longer: a page for each short story, in the
  background, five minutes at most; stories it didn't get to keep their
  summary.
- A story behind a paywall, or on a page built by scripts, keeps the feed's
  summary, and says so at its end.
- Only the story is kept: the page's section and byline lines over it, its
  headline said again, picture captions and boxes of code to embed it
  elsewhere are left out.
- **Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Whole stories** turns it off (a faster
  fetch, summaries only).

**Weather**: a box under the date shows now, today and the next two days,
each with a small drawing of its sky, the high and low, and the chance of
rain on a wet day. It comes with the day's paper:

- from your **Home Assistant** when the [House](House) tab's is connected (its
  first weather entity); nothing leaves your network;
- otherwise from **Open‑Meteo** (open-meteo.com: free, no account) for the
  town you set in **Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Weather → Town**, or on
  the setup page's Daily paper tab. **Degrees** switches Fahrenheit and
  Celsius (it starts as the town's country uses).

With no town set and no Home Assistant there's no box; **Weather on the front
page** turns it off.

**Today's calendar events**: when you use the [Calendar](Calendar) tab, the
front page has a **TODAY** box under the date with today's events, all-day
ones first, then by time. They're the events the Calendar tab last synced,
kept on the Kindle: nothing is fetched for it and nothing about them leaves
the Kindle. Tap the box for the Calendar tab. A day without events has no
box; **Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Today's calendar events** turns it off.

**Pictures**: an opened story shows its picture, whole, under its headline
(up to 24 stories an edition have one). The lead story's picture also runs
across the front page, a few more stories have theirs in the columns, and the
lead's is on the newspaper's front page on the shelf. They are the pictures
the feeds name for their stories (or, failing that, the one the story's own
page names), fetched with the paper, kept until the next edition and shown in
grey (in colour on a colour Kindle). Pictures between a story's paragraphs aren't shown.
**Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Pictures** turns them off
(the paper is fetched faster without them).

**On a colour Kindle** (Colorsoft, Scribe Colorsoft): each section's heading
is printed in its own colour (World red, Technology blue, Science green,
Books violet, Curiosities orange, Good news amber; a section of your own gets one of a few
others, always the same one), and the heavy rule under the nameplate, on the
paper and on Home, is the paper's red. Turning off KOReader's colour rendering
puts it back in black.

## The AI edition

**Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → AI edition** (or the tick
on the setup page's Daily paper tab). It needs the
[AI connection](Setup-Page-and-Connections#ai) to have a key; until then the
row is grey.

With it on, each paper fetched gets a short summary of every story (two to
four sentences), written by the AI service you set up, and the paper becomes
pages of articles: a headline, its picture when it has one, and its summary,
one article after another (two side by side on a big screen). Turn the pages
as usual. Open a story for the whole of it; it begins with **In brief (AI
summary)**. The date line says who wrote the summaries (*summaries by
Claude*).

- **What's sent:** when the paper is fetched, each story's headline and the
  first part of its text (about a thousand words at most) go to the AI
  service, one story at a time, about twenty a day. Nothing about you, your
  Kindle or your feeds' addresses is sent.
- **What it costs:** you pay the service by use. With Claude's small model,
  twenty short summaries a day come to a few cents a month.
- **When it starts:** with the next paper fetched (**Fetch again** at the
  paper's foot does it at once). Today's paper, fetched before, stays as it
  was.
- **When the service doesn't answer:** the stories it didn't answer for show
  how they begin, as in the ordinary paper. If no story got a summary (a
  refused key, no connection), the paper is the ordinary one that day; the
  log says why.
- **Full pages:** every page but the last is filled to its foot. Each
  column's pictures are shown taller until it's full, and what's left is
  shared evenly between its stories.
- A story of only a line or two isn't sent: it is its own summary.
- Summaries are written by a machine and can be wrong; the story itself is
  one tap away.
- Not on Kindles without Wi‑Fi.

## With keys

On a Kindle with keys, a frame shows where you are, and the 5-way moves it
**every way**: up and down a column, left and right between the columns,
along the foot (Fetch, Aa, Puzzles), and up to the TODAY box. **Press** opens
what's framed. When nothing lies further that way, right or down turns to the
next page and left or up to the one before; the page-turn keys always turn
the page.

A Kindle with a keyboard (Kindle 2, Keyboard, DX) also has a letter for
everything:

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| **Q W E R T Y U I O** (or 1–9) | Open the first to ninth story on the page. Each story shows its letter beside its byline. |
| **P** | Puzzles |
| **F** | Fetch the paper again |
| **D** | Today's events, in the Calendar tab (when the TODAY box is showing) |
| **Aa** | The type of the paper's pages |
| **Menu** | The quick panel |
| **Back** | Close the paper |

## Puzzles

Each day's paper comes with three puzzles you do on the Kindle: a
**sudoku**, a **word search** and a **maze**. Open the paper and choose
**Puzzles** at its foot (with keys: move the frame to it with the 5-way and
press; on a keyboard, **P**). The foot shows how many you've finished (**Puzzles 1/3**).

They are made on the Kindle, so they need no Wi‑Fi. The sudoku and the maze
come from the date alone. The word search hides **words from the paper's
stories** when there is a paper (see below), and words of its own when there
isn't.
What you've done is kept until the next day's paper (5 in the morning), when
new ones take their place.

The three are tabs across the top; tap one, or press a page-turn key.

| | Touch (finger or pen) | Keys |
|---|---|---|
| **Sudoku** | Tap a square, then a number under the grid. ⌫ rubs out, **✓?** checks, **Start again** clears what you wrote. | The 5-way moves. **Press** writes the next number (1 to 9, then empty). On a keyboard: **Q W E R T Y U I O** (the keys under 1–9) or the number keys write 1–9, **Del** rubs out, **C** checks, **N** starts again. |
| **Word search** | Draw along a word with a finger or the pen, from its first letter to its last (either way round), as you'd circle it on paper. Or tap its first letter, then its last. | The 5-way moves; **Press** on the first letter, then on the last. |
| **Maze** | Tap or swipe the way to go. **Start again** goes back to the start. | The 5-way goes that way; **N** starts again on a keyboard. |

- **Sudoku**: the printed numbers are on grey and can't be changed. Checking
  marks the squares that aren't right with a cut corner; nothing is marked
  until you ask. There is exactly one answer.
- **Word search**: eight words, across, down, diagonal, and some backwards.
  With a paper on the Kindle the words are taken from its stories, headline
  words first: plain words of four to eight letters, without everyday words
  (*through*, *people*, the days and months), without the news' grim ones,
  and only one of a family (*votes*, not also *voted*). The line under the
  letters says when the words are the paper's. The puzzle is made the first
  time you open it and kept for the day, so fetching the paper again doesn't
  change one you've started; one you haven't touched is made again from a
  newer paper. Feeds in other languages give fewer words (letters with
  accents are left out), and the puzzle's own list fills the gaps.
  Found words are crossed off the list and circled in the letters (on a
  colour Kindle their squares are also tinted, each word its own colour).
  The stroke doesn't have to be exact: it may wobble or run a little past
  the last letter. Nothing is drawn while your finger moves; the ring
  appears when you lift it. A stroke that isn't one of the words changes
  nothing and the page says so.
- **Maze**: in at the top left, out at the bottom right. One move goes on
  until a wall, a turning or a crossing. Dots show where you've been. Its
  size follows the screen, so it differs between Kindles.

**Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Puzzles** takes them off
the paper.

## When it's fetched

Once a day, the first time Home (or the paper) is on the screen after
5 in the morning by the Kindle's clock:

- With [Wi‑Fi](Wi-Fi) on **Auto**, GZreader turns Wi‑Fi on for it and off
  again afterwards. On **On** it's fetched straight away. On **Off** nothing
  is fetched until you ask: **Fetch today's paper** at the foot of the
  paper (or **Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Fetch now**), which turns
  Wi‑Fi on for that.
- If the feeds can't be reached, yesterday's paper stays (the widget says
  which day it's from) and GZreader tries again half an hour later.
- A feed that didn't answer is named at the end of the paper.

Stories older than a day and a half stay out, except from a feed that posts
seldom: its newest story is shown rather than nothing.

## Without Wi‑Fi

The Kindle 2, DX and DX Graphite (or any Kindle with **Sync through a
computer** chosen) get the paper each time you
[sync with a computer](Sync-with-a-Computer). Turn it on and choose feeds on
the Kindle as usual (Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper). Then,
when you sync, the sync page fetches the feeds and GZreader makes the paper
when it next starts.

A web page can only read a feed whose site allows it, and many news sites'
feeds don't (many blogs' do). The sync page names each feed it couldn't
read, and the paper is made from the others. With **Whole stories** on, the
page also tries each short story's own page, and only those whose sites
allow it come. There are no pictures and no weather box this way. Today's
calendar events still show, from the calendars the page brought.

## Choosing feeds

A feed is a site's list of its newest stories (RSS or Atom); most news sites
and blogs have one.

**From your phone or computer** (the easy way to paste addresses):
**Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Set up from a browser (QR code)** opens the
[setup page](Setup-Page-and-Connections) at its **Daily paper** tab. Scan the
code, or open the address shown on a computer on the same Wi‑Fi. There you
can turn the paper on or off, take it off the bookshelf, tick the feeds
GZreader comes with, paste your own (one per line, as in `feeds.txt` below),
and remove them. **Save**, then press Back on the Kindle.

**On the Kindle**: **Settings → Accounts and services → Daily paper → Feeds**:

- GZreader comes with a few to tick, by section: BBC News (World, Technology,
  Science), NPR, Ars Technica, NASA, Literary Hub, Atlas Obscura, NPR Strange
  News, and under Good news, Good News Network and Positive News; Hacker
  News, NPR Science and Reasons to be Cheerful are there unticked.
- **Add a feed**: type its address, and if you like a name and a section
  (your feeds go under **Your feeds** otherwise).
- **Add from feeds.txt**: put a file `feeds.txt` in the GZreader folder (or at
  the top of the Kindle's USB drive) with one feed per line:

  ```
  https://blog.example.com/feed
  Knitting weekly | https://knitting.example.com/rss
  Crafts | Knitting weekly | https://knitting.example.com/rss
  ```

  An address alone, `Name | address`, or `Section | Name | address`. Lines
  starting with `#` are skipped.
- Hold one of your own feeds to remove it; untick it to leave it out for now.

After a change, the paper is made again the next time Home is shown.

## Privacy

Fetching a feed is a plain request to that feed's site, as opening the site
in a browser would be: the site sees the Kindle's internet address and that
GZreader asked. Nothing else is sent: no account, no cookies, nothing about
you or what you read. Nothing goes through anyone else's server. With
**Pictures** on, a story's picture is fetched the same way from wherever the
feed says it is, often a picture server on another site than the feed's.
With **Whole stories** on, each short story's own page is fetched too. It's
usually on the feed's site, but not always (Hacker News links anywhere); that
site then sees the Kindle's internet address as well. Story addresses are not
written to the log.
For the weather, Open‑Meteo is sent the town's name once (to find where it
is) and after that only its coordinates rounded to two decimals, about a
kilometre, with each day's request; it sees the Kindle's internet address
too. The town and the coordinates are never written to the log. Home
Assistant's weather stays on your network. Feed
addresses are never written to the [diagnostic log](Privacy-and-Diagnostics#diagnostic-log)
(only the site's name), as some carry a personal key.
